---
title: "Effectivity for the Subsets of Exact Measure"
---

As discussed in Section [%s](#section:subsets_of_exact_measure), we have extened Besicovitch's result about finding compact subsets of finite, non-zero $s$-measure to sufficiently nice nets in our Theorem [%s](#theorem:existence_of_compact_subset_exact_for_premeasure). The proof of this result relies on a density result: Proposition [%s](#proposition:full_density_of_U_and_S).

The **$n$-thin** sequences defined in our proof play the crucial role of helping pass from $\mathcal{U}^c_n$ to $\mathcal{U}^c_{n _ 1}$, as shown in Lemma [%s](#lemma:use_n_thin_to_pass_n_to_n+1). They have the special property that any length-$n$ string used in a cover of $E_Z$ may be replaced by proper extensions to make another cover of $E_Z$ not significantly greater in its direct $\rho_h$-weight. The $n$-thin sequences were shown to be dense in any non-empty $\mathcal{S}^d_{n_0}$ whenever $n \geq n_0$ in Lemma [%s](#lemma:n_thins_dense_in_complement). We suspect that the $n$-thin sequences should be useful in other contexts involving making refinements to optimal covers.

One could also imagine effectivizing the proof of Theorem [%s](#theorem:existence_of_compact_subset_exact_for_premeasure) as follows: finding an oracle $B \in 2^\omega$ such that, if $F \subseteq 2^\omega$ were in fact a $\Pi^0_1$-class, $B$ is strong enough to compute a code $Z \in \mathcal{P}_F$ for which $E_Z$ satisfies the desired properties, putting $E_Z \in \Pi^0_1(B)$. One might even modify $\phi_{n, m}(Z)$ to instead optimize over the **a priori** discrepancy $\operatorname{\Delta M}_s(\sigma)$ over the strings for which $Z(\sigma) = 1$. Then, an appropriate lightface version (i.e., correspondence principle) of Lutz and Miller's point-to-set principle [%s](#theorem:point_to_set_s_measure) would relate this function back to the Hausdorff $s$-measure of the compact subset $E_Z$ coded by $Z$. In particular, we hope for an effective proof of Besicovitch's result over Cantor space via lower-semicomputable continuous semimeasures.
