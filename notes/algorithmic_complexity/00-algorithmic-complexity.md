---
title: "Algorithmic Complexity"
label: chapter:algorithmic_complexity
---

This chapter reviews the basic definitions and results of algorithmic information theory over finitary objects, like the natural numbers or finite binary strings. We also introduce the fundamental quantities of geometric measure theory. In particular, this chapter lays the groundwork for understanding the zoo of approaches to evaluating effective fractal dimension which all happen agree.

- [Measures and Hausdorff Dimension](#section:Measures_and_Hausdorff_Dimension): premeasures, the outer measures they induce, Hausdorff measures and dimension, packing dimension, and the Hausdorff dimensions of a measure.
- [Computability Theory](#section:Computability_Theory): Turing machines, computable analysis, and the lightface hierarchies.
- [Prefix Complexity](#section:Prefix_Comlexity): plain and prefix Kolmogorov complexity, the chain rule, and the Kraft Inequality.
- [Algorithmic Randomness](#section:Algorithmic_Randomness): weights, tests, and covers, the test-based approach to (partial) randomness.
- [Semimeasures](#section:Semimeasures): discrete and continuous semimeasures and supergales as an alternative measure of information content.
- [Complexity Characterizations of Algorithmic Randomness](#section:Complexity_Characterizations_of_Algorithmic_Randomness): randomness in terms of the discrepancy between a string's algorithmic entropy and its mass.
